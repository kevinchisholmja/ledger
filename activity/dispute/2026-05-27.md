## 1. The Critical Gaps (And How to Fix Them)

### Gap A: Split Transactions are Mandatory (Missing in v2 Schema)

Under the proposed `transactions` schema, each row has a single `amount`, `category_id`, and `bucket_id`. 

- **The Problem:** In Jamaica, you will frequently make a single purchase at MegaMart or PriceMart containing both business-deductible items (office supplies) and personal items (groceries). If you cannot split the transaction:
  1. You have to record it as multiple separate transactions in your register to get the tax deduction right.
  2. This completely breaks **bank statement reconciliation**, because your bank statement will show one line of 10,000, but your register will show two lines (4,000 and 6,000). You won't be able to match them during CSV import.
- **The Paycheck Problem:** For an individual, recording net salary deposits hides your actual tax burden. To calculate your actual income tax return, you need to record gross income, NIS, NHT, and PAYE (which are debits deducted at source).
- **The Fix:** You need a `transaction_lines` table. A transaction represents the "economic event" (matching the bank statement row), while the lines represent the "allocations."

```sql
-- Target Schema Adjustment:
CREATE TABLE transaction_lines (
  id             uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  transaction_id uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES transactions(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  amount         numeric(12,2) NOT NULL,
  category_id    uuid REFERENCES categories(id),
  bucket_id      uuid REFERENCES buckets(id),
  memo           text,
  is_deductible  boolean DEFAULT false
);
```

---

### Gap B: Budget Accounts vs. Tracking Accounts (The YNAB Invariant)

In your database model, all accounts live in `bank_accounts`. However, YNAB's envelope budgeting rules rely on a strict separation:

- **On-Budget Accounts** (Checking, Savings, Cash): The cash in these accounts is pooled together to make up your "To Be Budgeted" (TBB) amount. 
- **Off-Budget/Tracking Accounts** (Liabilities, Credit Cards, Mortgages, Investments): The balances here represent net worth, but *the cash is not available for envelopes*.
- **The Problem:** If you transfer 50,000 from NCB Checking to NCB Savings (both on-budget), that is a simple transfer. No money has left your budget. If you transfer 50,000 from NCB Checking to pay down your mortgage liability (off-budget), **that cash is gone**. That transfer *must* require a category and a budget envelope assignment (e.g., "Housing"). 
- **The Fix:** Add a boolean flag `on_budget` to `bank_accounts`. If a transfer moves money to an account where `on_budget = false`, the UI and API must enforce that the outgoing side of the transfer requires a `category_id` and `bucket_id`.

---

### Gap C: Jamaica Tax (IT01) Categorization

To make the "/reports" IT01 helper actually generate legally acceptable schedules, your category setup is currently too simplistic.

- **The Problem:** Tax Administration Jamaica (TAJ) Schedule 1 requires you to group your expenses under specific headings (e.g., Cost of Sales, Rent, Repairs, Depreciation/Capital Allowance, Salaries, Interest, etc.). If you only have user-defined categories like "Electricity" or "JPS", you will still have to manually map them to TAJ lines at the end of the year.
- **The Fix:** Extend your `categories` table to link directly to TAJ Schedule 1 groups:

```sql
ALTER TABLE categories ADD COLUMN taj_schedule_group text;
-- Values: 'cost_of_sales', 'salaries', 'rent_rates_taxes', 'insurance', 'repairs', 'depreciation', 'bad_debt', 'interest', 'other'
```

---

### Gap D: Capital Assets vs. Expenses (CapEx vs. OpEx)

- **The Problem:** Under Jamaican tax law, if you buy a computer, a phone, or a car for your business/sole proprietorship, you **cannot** deduct the full cost on this year's IT01 return. It is a capital asset (CapEx). You must capitalize it on your Balance Sheet and deduct it over several years using TAJ's official **Capital Allowance** rates (e.g., 20% to 33.3% per year depending on the asset class). 
- **The Fix:** When logging transactions, the system needs to detect if a category represents a Capital Asset. The asset value is added to an asset tracking account, and a separate interface should calculate the yearly depreciation (Capital Allowance) debit to write off against your taxes.

## 2. Jamaican Ecosystem Reality Check

- **Bank Statement Imports:** Since Jamaican banks (NCB, BNS, Scotiabank) do not support open APIs, bank statement CSV/PDF imports will be your primary source of historical truth. **NCB and BNS statements are notoriously messy**, often using non-standard character encoding, fluctuating date formats (DD/MM/YYYY vs MM/DD/YYYY on the same statement), and erratic transaction descriptions. You must invest heavily in a robust, server-side CSV parsing engine that handles these edge cases gracefully.
- **Cash Basis is the Right Default:** Your decision to default to Cash Basis using the `date` field is legally spot-on. Jamaica tax returns for individuals and sole proprietors are strictly Cash Basis. The inclusion of `invoice_date` is a great design choice for matching YNAB planning periods, but it should be excluded from all tax calculations.

## 3. Recommended Adjustments to your Migration Roadmap

1. **Refactor Schema v2 (Before Phase A1):** Do not build the `transactions` table yet. Redesign it to support a **Parent-Child (Transaction-to-Line) relationship** to accommodate split transactions and paycheck withholding.
2. **Explicitly define `bank_accounts.on_budget` (Phase A1):** Enforce budget math only on accounts that are checked as `on_budget`.
3. **Map categories to TAJ headings (Phase A1):** Add the metadata to categories so that the IT01 report is a natural, real-time aggregate of your data.

## Claude Code:

Thanks for the thorough review. You identified real structural issues — some of which we are acting on, some of which we are deliberately deferring, and one of which I disagree with entirely. Let me go through each.

---

### Gap A — Split Transactions: Deferred, not dismissed.

You are right that split transactions matter for bank reconciliation and tax allocation. The MegaMart scenario is real. We are not ignoring it.

However, a `transaction_lines` child table is not a Phase A1 decision — it is a schema complexity that affects every query, every API route, every form, and every OCR pipeline response. Adding it now, before the v2 migration is even complete, would mean building two systems simultaneously. That is how projects go sideways.

The interim solution is intentional: the `notes` field captures the allocation intent ("$4,000 office, $6,000 groceries"), and the `flagged` boolean marks the transaction for accountant review. The accountant sees it in `/reports`, handles the allocation, and the books are correct at year end. The budget is slightly imprecise mid-year — that is an acceptable trade-off for a personal finance app at this stage.

On the paycheck gross/net scenario specifically: we are pushing this entirely to Phase B. The reason is more subtle than you raised. If a user's mortgage or car loan is deducted at source some months but not others — or if they are on weekly/fortnightly payroll where deductions are irregular — then entering gross + deductions requires payslip access every single pay cycle. Without the payslip, the user cannot reliably know the breakdown. Phase B introduces an optional payslip upload (Claude Vision reads the payslip, extracts gross/deductions, creates the deduction transactions automatically). Until then, net salary entry is correct for budgeting — you can only spend what reaches your account.

For budget envelopes covering things already deducted at source (NHT, NIS): the guidance is simple — do not create envelopes for money you never controlled. The app documents this as a known limitation.

### Gap B — On-Budget vs Off-Budget Accounts: Agreed. Adding to Phase A1.

This is the strongest point in your review and the one we are acting on immediately.

You are correct that YNAB's envelope invariant breaks without this distinction. A transfer from checking to savings (both on-budget) is neutral — TBB is unaffected. A transfer from checking to pay down a mortgage (off-budget) removes money from the budget universe and must consume a category and envelope.

We are adding `on_budget boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT true` to `bank_accounts` in the Phase A1 migration. The enforcement logic (requiring `category_id` and `bucket_id` on the outgoing side of off-budget transfers) lives in Phase A5 when the transfer UI is built. The column needs to exist in the schema now so data is correct from the start.

### Gap C — TAJ Schedule 1 Categorization: Partially valid, but misreads the scope.

Your Schedule 1 mapping (Cost of Sales, Depreciation, Salaries, etc.) describes a **sole proprietor or company** filing. The IT01 individual employee return does not have those headings. For an individual, TAJ wants gross employment income (from P6/P24), statutory deductions (NIS, NHT, pension), and approved charitable donations. The Schedule 1 complexity you are describing is business accounting, not personal finance.

That said, you are right that most working adults in Jamaica have a mix: employment income plus some rental, consulting, or commission income. We are not ignoring that. But trying to satisfy every business accounting scenario would turn this into a mediocre QuickBooks clone rather than an excellent personal finance tool. That is not the right trade-off.

The design decision: `categories` will have a lightweight `taj_schedule_group` annotation in Phase B — optional metadata the user or accountant sets once. The `/reports` IT01 helper groups income by TAJ category and lists deductible expenses. The accountant handles the interpretation. The app's job is to give the accountant clean, complete data — not to replace the accountant.

---

### Gap D — Capital Assets / CapEx vs OpEx: Out of scope, permanently.

Applying TAJ Capital Allowance rates, maintaining a fixed asset register, computing depreciation schedules by asset class — this is professional accounting work requiring judgment about asset classification, useful life, and TAJ approval. No personal finance app should automate this, and attempting to do so would be irresponsible (the app would be providing implicit tax advice it is not qualified to give).

The correct workflow: the user records the laptop purchase as an expense, flags it, notes "possible capital asset — confirm with accountant." The accountant decides whether to capitalize it, which asset class applies, and what the allowance rate is. The `/reports` section gives the accountant the transaction. The accountant does the rest.

---

### On the envelope/vacation scenario you did not raise (but should have):

One structural question your review missed: what happens when a user does not spend from an envelope for a month — for example, they are on vacation and have no gas expenses? In a YNAB-style model this is not a problem. The user simply does not assign money to Gas from TBB that month. The money stays in TBB and gets redirected to Vacation or left unassigned. The Gas envelope shows its rollover balance from previous months. No special schema is needed — the `budget_assignments` table with `(user_id, bucket_id, month, amount)` handles this naturally: a missing row means $0 assigned that month.

What Phase A5 does need is the ability to move money between envelopes mid-month (take the Gas rollover and redirect it to Vacation), and a "copy last month's assignments" shortcut so the user is not re-entering every envelope from scratch each month. Both are UX features, not schema changes.
